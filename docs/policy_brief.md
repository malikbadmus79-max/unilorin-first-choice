# Policy brief: the University of Ilorin's lost first choice

As of 31 October 2026. Evidence and sources: [findings.md](findings.md).

## The issue

The University of Ilorin ranked first for first-choice UTME applications in every year from 2018 to 2024 for which this study has figures. Between 2020 and 2026 its first-choice applications fell from 103,582 to 52,514 and its rank from first to seventh, while all six of its peers in JAMB's top 10 in both 2021 and 2026 gained applicants. From 2022 it drew about a third as many of the country's qualified candidates to its screening, relative to the national pool, as it had in 2021 (10.8 per 100 candidates scoring 200 or more in 2021; 3.6 in 2024).

## What the evidence shows

1. The fall after 2021 is specific to the university. The 2021 fall followed a national fall in UTME scores.
2. Its timing matches the 2022 national strike, the second the university joined after 2020, when it struck for the first time in 20 years. Universities that stayed open in 2022 gained: Lagos State University in applications and search interest, Kwara State University in search interest (its applications are not published); federal peers that also struck were not penalised.
3. New private universities and insecurity do not explain the fall. The available fee data does not support fees as a cause, but rests on one outlet.
4. Competition for places has eased: about 1.3 candidates attended its screening per admission in 2024, against about 3.4 in 2020, while admissions held at about 13,000.

## Why it matters

- For the university: it now selects from a smaller pool of qualified applicants. Fewer candidates with high UTME scores may affect the academic profile of incoming cohorts over time.
- For candidates and parents: the university's reputation as hard to enter is out of date. In 2024/2025 it admitted 12,856 students, while 16,097 candidates attended its screening.
- For federal policy: when a university's distinctive advantage is lost, candidates respond quickly. Calendar stability is valued and measurable.

## Recommendations

For the University of Ilorin

1. Publish its admission figures every year in one place: first-choice applicants, candidates scoring 180 or more, candidates screened, places, offers and matriculations. The figures now come from speeches and press reports and cannot all be reconciled.
2. Publish its academic calendar and record of disruption, so that candidates can compare it with other universities on evidence rather than reputation.
3. Survey applicants who declined offers or chose other universities, to establish their reasons. This study cannot do so.
4. Communicate current admission chances to candidates. Its reputation for being hard to enter may now discourage qualified applicants.

For JAMB

5. Publish first-choice applications for every institution each year, not only the top 10, in a machine-readable file. This would allow trends for universities such as Kwara State University to be measured.
6. Publish candidate numbers by state of origin on a consistent basis across years.

For the Federal Ministry of Education

7. Track academic calendar disruption by university as a published indicator, alongside admission demand.

## Limits

The evidence shows timing and patterns, not candidates' reasons. Most figures are JAMB or university statements reported in the press, and JAMB's own documents could not be retrieved. There is no comparable 2022 figure for the university.
